A ground-beef sheet-pan meatloaf dinner adapted for venison: a free-form loaf shaped right on the pan (no loaf tin, no rolling meatballs) and roasted with potatoes and carrots under a sweet ketchup glaze. Grated carrot, milk-soaked oats and a little olive oil keep the lean venison moist. Serves 2 adults + kids. **Hands-on: about 14 minutes · Total: about 50 minutes**.

**Thaw ground venison in the fridge the day before.** Recipe assumes a 1 lb package; for 1½ lb, scale the loaf ingredients by 1½, shape a longer loaf, and add 5–10 minutes.

## Ingredients

### Meatloaf
- 1 lb ground venison, thawed
- ½ cup old-fashioned rolled oats (or ½ cup plain breadcrumbs)
- ⅓ cup whole milk
- 1 large egg
- 1 medium carrot (organic), finely grated on a box grater (about ½ cup)
- 1 oz block Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated (about ⅓ cup)
- 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce (optional)
- ½ teaspoon garlic powder
- ½ teaspoon onion powder
- ½ teaspoon dried oregano or thyme
- ½ teaspoon salt

### Glaze
- ¼ cup organic ketchup
- 1 teaspoon maple syrup or brown sugar

### Vegetables
- 1½ lb baby or small Yukon Gold potatoes (organic), halved (quartered if large)
- 3 medium carrots (organic), peeled and cut into 2-inch sticks
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- ¼ teaspoon salt

## Instructions
1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Line a large rimmed sheet pan with parchment.
2. In a large bowl, stir the oats and milk together and let them sit while you prep. Toss the potatoes and carrots with 2 tablespoons oil and ¼ teaspoon salt right on the sheet pan, then push them to the two ends.
3. Add the egg, grated carrot, Parmesan, olive oil, Worcestershire, garlic and onion powders, herbs and salt to the oat mixture and stir. Add the venison and mix gently with your hands just until combined. Don't overwork it.
4. Shape into a loaf about 8 × 4 inches and 1½ inches tall in the middle of the pan. Stir together the ketchup and maple syrup and spread it over the top.
5. Roast 30–35 minutes, until the center of the loaf reads 160°F and the potatoes are tender. Venison goes from juicy to dry fast, so start checking at 28 minutes and pull it at 160°F.
6. Rest the loaf 5 minutes, then slice.

## Leftovers, Freezing & Tips
- **Leftovers:** keep 4 days in the fridge. Cold or warmed slices make great sandwiches for lunch.
- **Freezing:** freeze sliced, cooked meatloaf for up to 3 months; reheat covered at 350°F. Or shape the raw loaf, wrap it tightly and freeze; thaw in the fridge overnight before baking.
- Want a green veg too? Add 8 oz green beans to the pan for the last 15 minutes.
- Adapted from a classic ground-beef meatloaf; venison swaps 1:1.

## Baby Adaptations
- Meatloaf is soft and easy for toddlers: cut it into thin strips or small cubes for younger kids, and serve the potatoes and carrots cooked very soft (lightly smash them with a fork).
- Scrape off most of the glaze for younger kids' portions to keep sugar and salt low. Cool to warm.
- Contains egg, dairy (milk, Parmesan), and possibly wheat (use oats rather than breadcrumbs to avoid it; check the oats are certified gluten-free if needed).
