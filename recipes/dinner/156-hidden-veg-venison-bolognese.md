A classic weeknight ground-beef bolognese adapted for ground venison, with carrot, zucchini and onion blitzed into the sauce so they melt away. Serves 2 adults + kids, with leftovers. **Hands-on: about 14 minutes · Total: about 40 minutes** (the sauce simmers on its own while the pasta cooks).

**Thaw ground venison in the fridge the day before.** Recipe assumes a 1 lb package; for 12 oz use it as written, for 1½–2 lb add a second jar of passata and an extra carrot.

## Ingredients
- 1 lb ground venison, thawed
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil (venison is lean, so don't skip it)
- 1 small yellow onion, peeled and quartered
- 2 medium carrots (organic), peeled and cut in chunks
- 1 small zucchini (organic), cut in chunks
- 2 cloves garlic, peeled
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 1 jar (24 oz) Jovial organic tomato passata (strained tomatoes)
- ½ cup whole milk
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 bay leaf (optional)
- ¼–½ teaspoon salt (light for kids; adults season at the table)
- 12 oz pasta (rigatoni, penne or spaghetti)
- 1 oz block Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated at the table (about ½ cup)

## Instructions
1. Put a large pot of water on to boil for the pasta.
2. Pulse onion, carrots, zucchini and garlic in a food processor until very finely chopped (10–12 pulses). No food processor: grate the carrot and zucchini on a box grater and finely chop the onion. This takes a few minutes longer.
3. Heat the oil in a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the vegetables and a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, until soft, about 5 minutes.
4. Push the veg to the side, add the venison and break it into small crumbles with a spatula. Cook just until no longer pink, 3–4 minutes. Don't brown it hard, because lean venison dries out.
5. Stir in tomato paste, passata, milk, oregano, bay leaf and salt. Bring to a gentle bubble, cover partly, and simmer on low for 20–25 minutes, stirring once or twice. The sauce should reach a full simmer, so the meat goes past 160°F. Splash in pasta water if it gets too thick.
6. Meanwhile cook the pasta in salted water according to the package. Drain, keeping ½ cup water. Remove the bay leaf, toss the pasta with the sauce, and loosen with pasta water as needed. Grate Parmesan over the top.

## Leftovers, Freezing & Tips
- **Leftovers:** keep 4 days in the fridge; reheat to steaming (165°F).
- **Freezing:** the sauce (without pasta) freezes well for up to 3 months. Doubling it makes a second dinner for almost no extra work.
- Adapted from a ground-beef bolognese. Venison swaps 1:1; the extra oil and the milk keep it tender.

## Baby Adaptations
- For younger kids, choose a small pasta shape (ditalini, small shells) or cut pasta into ½-inch pieces; the crumbled sauce is already soft and toddler-safe.
- Cool to warm before serving; skip extra salt and cheese on their portions or keep them very light.
- Contains wheat (pasta) and dairy (milk, Parmesan).
